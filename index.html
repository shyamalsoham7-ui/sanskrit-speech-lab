Sanskrit Speech Lab

MFCC-based pronunciation assessment and HMM-based Gita shloka recognition. Everything runs in your browser; audio never leaves your device.

Setup

Record the reference once (teacher or a good speaker); it is remembered in this browser. Then record or upload the student attempt.

Evaluation

Leave-one-out test: each sample is held out, models are retrained without it, and the sample is classified. Needs at least 2 verses and a verse with 2+ samples.

Pipeline

Features. 16 kHz audio, pre-emphasis 0.97, 25 ms Hamming frames with 10 ms hop, 512-point FFT, 26 mel filters, log, DCT to 13 MFCCs, silence trimming, cepstral mean normalization. Spectral centroid, zero-crossing rate, energy and duration are tracked too.

Pronunciation. Dynamic time warping aligns student to reference on MFCC c1–c12. The mean aligned distance d becomes a similarity of 100·exp(−(d/strictness)²). Per-segment cost, per-coefficient difference, duration and brightness show what differs.

Verses. One left-to-right HMM per verse (20 states, diagonal Gaussians, self-loop and advance transitions), trained by Viterbi re-estimation (segmental k-means) from your enrolled samples. A test recording is scored against every model by length-normalized Viterbi log-likelihood; the best score is the recognized verse.

Limits: models are speaker-dependent when trained on one voice; the similarity score is a calibrated heuristic, not a phonetic ground truth. Use the strictness slider to calibrate against teacher judgments.
